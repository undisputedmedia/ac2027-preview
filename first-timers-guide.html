FIRST-TIMER’S GUIDE

YOUR FIRST CONVENTION

Ten things to know before your first Antiochian Archdiocese Convention: what the week is for, who it’s for, and how to plan it. The 2027 Convention is July 19–25 at the Hilton Anaheim.

Updated November 2026

THE SHORT VERSION

A week of worship, family and the whole Archdiocese

Every two years, clergy, delegates and families from parishes across North America spend a week together under one roof.

The 58th Biennial Antiochian Archdiocese Convention is hosted by Saint Andrew Orthodox Christian Church, Riverside, California, and held at the Hilton Anaheim. It is the first Archdiocese Convention in California since 2009.

You don’t need to be a delegate, and you don’t need to be Antiochian. If it’s your first time, start here.

TEN THINGS TO KNOW

Before you go

1. It’s a week of worship first

Divine Services are the heart of the week, with the hierarchs and clergy of the Archdiocese serving in a chapel set up inside the hotel. Everything else on the schedule is planned around them.

2. Everyone is welcome

Delegates are seated for the General Assembly, but the Convention is open to everyone: families, young adults, Orthodox Christians from any jurisdiction, and anyone curious about the Orthodox faith. You don’t need to be Antiochian. More about the Convention.

3. The General Assembly is one part of the week

The General Assembly is the largest legislative body of the Archdiocese and meets every two years. Parish delegates are seated there, and the work of the Archdiocese is discussed and decided. If you aren’t a delegate, the rest of the week is still yours.

4. Bring the kids

Teen SOYO and Kidz Club (ages 4–12) run all week alongside the adult program. Youth badges need a Parent Consent form and, for teens, the Teen Code of Conduct on file at check-in, so have both ready.

5. Watch the Bible Bowl and the Oratorical Festival

Two of the Archdiocese’s youth traditions come to the Convention: the Bible Bowl, and the Oratorical Festival, where young speakers from across the dioceses present. Both are open to watch and easy to love.

6. Visit the Kursk Root Icon

A shrine for the Kursk Root Icon of the Mother of God is part of the week, so you can venerate the icon between sessions.

7. Pack for the chapel and the banquet

Plan on modest church clothes for services and sessions, and something festive for the Grand Banquet. Southern California in July is warm, but meeting rooms are air-conditioned, so bring a layer.

8. Wear your badge

Your Convention badge is your ticket: it is required for every non-liturgical event. The registration desk is open daily from 10:00 AM to 9:00 PM and closes during services.

9. Stay in the block

Every session is inside the Hilton Anaheim, so staying in the Convention block means walking downstairs instead of driving in. The rate is $209 a night for a King or two Queens, from July 17 to July 25. Reserve your room, and read the travel guide for airports and parking.

10. Registration opens later in 2026

Registration runs through the Archdiocese, not through this site. Read how registration works, and get notified the day it opens.
