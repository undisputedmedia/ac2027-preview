THE ANTIOCHIAN ORTHODOX CHURCH

TEN THINGS TO KNOW

Who hosts the Convention? The Antiochian Orthodox Christian Archdiocese of North America, part of one of the oldest churches in the world. Here are ten things to know before July 19–25, 2027.

Updated November 2026

IN BRIEF

An apostolic church, at home in North America

The Church of Antioch traces its founding to the Apostles Peter and Paul. Its Archdiocese in North America gathers every two years at the Convention.

The 58th Biennial Antiochian Archdiocese Convention meets July 19–25, 2027 at the Hilton Anaheim, hosted by Saint Andrew Orthodox Christian Church, Riverside, California. About the Convention.

TEN THINGS

Ten things to know

1. It’s where the name “Christian” began

“The disciples were first called Christians in Antioch” (Acts 11:26). The Church of Antioch traces its founding to the Apostles Peter, its first bishop, and Paul.

2. Its patriarch sits in Damascus

After Antioch fell in the 13th century, the patriarchal seat moved to Damascus in the 14th century, where it remains. His Beatitude JOHN X was elected Patriarch of Antioch and All the East in December 2012.

3. Its North American story starts with a saint

St. Raphael of Brooklyn arrived in the United States in 1895 to serve Arabic-speaking Orthodox Christians. In 1904 he became the first Orthodox bishop consecrated in North America. He reposed in 1915 and was glorified as a saint in 2000.

4. One Archdiocese since 1975

The Archdioceses of New York and Toledo were reunited in 1975 into a single Antiochian Archdiocese of North America, led for decades by Metropolitan PHILIP.

5. Self-ruled since 2003

In 2003 the Holy Synod of Antioch granted the Archdiocese self-rule. Its General Assembly, the largest legislative body of the Archdiocese, meets at the Convention every two years.

6. Led today by Metropolitan SABA

His Eminence Metropolitan SABA was elected in February 2023 and enthroned at St. Nicholas Cathedral in Brooklyn on May 13, 2023. The 2027 Convention is held with his blessing.

7. More than 260 parishes and missions

Across the United States and Canada, the Archdiocese has more than 260 parishes and missions in eight dioceses. Southern California belongs to the Diocese of Los Angeles and the West, shepherded by Bishop ANTHONY.

8. Deep roots in Southern California

In 1987 the Archdiocese received more than 2,000 members of the Evangelical Orthodox Church, with the first chrismations at St. Michael in Van Nuys. St. Michael of Van Nuys went on to host the 2009 Convention in Palm Desert.

9. St. Raphael rests at Antiochian Village

The Archdiocese’s camp and conference center near Ligonier, Pennsylvania, opened its first camp season in 1979. St. Raphael is buried beside its outdoor chapel.

10. Four organizations you’ll meet at the Convention

The Fellowship of St. John the Divine, Teen SOYO, the Antiochian Women and the Order of St. Ignatius of Antioch are all part of Archdiocese life, and you’ll see their work throughout Convention week. See the schedule.
